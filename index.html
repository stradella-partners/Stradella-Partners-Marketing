The Strategy At most one trade per day

Every trading day at 1:00 PM Eastern, the Hybrid Engine asks one question: has the market established a direction worth trading for the rest of the afternoon? Two independent signal systems answer it. The LC engine (lunch continuation) measures whether the morning's move continued or reversed through the lunch session, a relative, ratio-based read. The VW engine (velocity window) measures absolute price velocity across fixed midday windows, a magnitude-based read. Each engine evaluates four distinct signal paths (continuation and fade, early and late), and a simple arbitration rule picks at most one trade per day: when both engines agree on direction, the earlier entry wins; when they disagree, the velocity engine, the better direction-reader, takes precedence.

Signals enter between 1:45 and 2:45 PM in 3x leveraged S&P 500 ETFs (SPXL long / SPXS short), and every position is closed by 3:55 PM. No positions are ever held overnight or through a weekend. Scheduled macro-event days (CPI, FOMC, non-farm payrolls) and half-day sessions are sat out by rule.

Position sizing is fixed and mechanical. Every trade deploys four dollars of gross exposure for every dollar of capital, 4x intraday margin, into 3x leveraged S&P 500 ETFs, which means up to 12x instantaneous market exposure while in a position, and zero the moment the book is flat. Exposure never compounds and is never discretionary: the same dollar size on every trade, win or lose, with the broker's margin used only intraday and no capital committed overnight.

The system trades both directions with near-symmetric results and sits out roughly 40–50% of sessions entirely.

Discipline Flat at every close

Most trading-strategy risk lives overnight. The Hybrid Engine simply never holds it.

Gaps, weekend headlines, halted opens: the book is flat at every close, so each day starts fresh.

Inside the trade, risk is bounded by engineering, not hope: a hard stop on every position, resting at the broker from the moment of entry (it executes even if our systems go dark), plus profit-protecting trailing stops, time-based profit tapers, a volatility-regime gate that stands the slower engine down in disorderly markets, and an independent end-of-day watchdog that force-flattens anything still open. In the five-year backtest at the live sizing, the worst single trade cost -5.2% of capital, the worst month was -26% of capital (-6.4% per dollar of exposure), and the maximum drawdown was -33.8% of capital, fully recovered within 26 trades, with zero down years in all five. Every one of those figures is quoted at 4x because that is how the strategy actually trades.

Year Two · +193.9% · the outlier

Year Two captured the back half of the 2022 bear market, the single best environment for this strategy: large morning moves and strong afternoon follow-through occurred two to three times more often than in a normal year, generating roughly 40% more trades at a steady win rate. Because the strategy shorts as readily as it buys, the down-trending days were fully tradeable, making 2022-style volatility the upside case while the median year remains the planning number.

Methodology
01
Canonical data
Full consolidated-tape (SIP) 1-minute bars, 2021–2026, audited for calendar, DST, splits, and gaps; complete NYSE session coverage.
02
Deterministic replication
Clean-room engine reproduced the production system trade-for-trade; every headline run hashes identically on repeat execution.
03
Pre-registered experiments
Ablations, parameter surfaces, and acceptance rules fixed before results were computed; ~1,225 configurations logged in a testing registry.
04
Placebo controls
The strategy beat 100% of 400 matched random-day and direction-flip placebos; inverting its logic loses -119%; the edge is signal, not market drift.
05
Walk-forward validation
Expanding-window out-of-sample testing across 2022–2026; the production parameters were re-selected in every window: a stability plateau, not a lucky point.
06
Robustness
Block-bootstrap 5th percentile still +92%; results survive delayed entries, missed fills, ±10% parameter perturbation, and 3x the measured trading costs.
